# Technical interviewing: from candidate conversations to hiring systems

I have interviewed software developers since 2011 and conducted interviews professionally in English
since 2020. It began when a manager invited me to interview candidates after recognizing my
interpersonal skills. I enjoyed the work and introduced more deliberate preparation: questions,
practical tests and a clearer basis for evaluation.

Today, my independent technical interviewing and hiring consulting is part-time freelance work,
scheduled outside my primary working hours, including early mornings and weekends. I have conducted
more than 1,500 developer interviews over five years, including 394 in a single year. My work extends
from individual interviews to assessment design, interviewer training, hiring workflows and client
calibration.

## Understanding the work before designing the interview

I work with hiring managers to translate business needs into realistic technical requirements,
interview guides and evaluation criteria. This includes challenging mismatches between the actual
work, the requested seniority and the hiring budget. Extensive experience, full-stack delivery,
AI expertise and external client deadlines call for compensation that reflects that scope.

Calibration continues after interviews. In one engagement, inconsistent feedback revealed that a
Kotlin requirement concerned backend development rather than mobile development. Bringing the client
back into the conversation helped clarify what the assessment needed to measure.

## Assessing roles beyond my primary engineering stack

My work has included software engineers, QA engineers, SDETs, forward-deployed engineers, product
roles and leadership candidates, including two CTO candidates for one vacancy. I have prepared guides
and conducted interviews for areas including Adobe Workfront, staff-level Elixir, principal-level
.NET, Scala, Rust and frontend development.

This does not mean I have professional implementation experience in every technology I assess. I
combine client discovery, research, AI-assisted preparation and validation by experienced specialists.
For a Rust assessment, I used AI to explore requirements and evaluation criteria, then had a Rust
specialist review and approve the exercise. The objective is a defensible process that other
interviewers can also use.

I have used accessible online environments such as Replit, CodeShare and OneCompiler to prepare and
share exercises when budgets were limited.

## Designing and validating assessments

I have used HackerRank since 2023 and also work with Coderbyte, creating questions, configuring tests,
reviewing results and comparing assessment variants. I have created more than 50 assessments and
over 100 interview guides and evaluation processes. Peer trials help check whether exercises are
clear, feasible and relevant before they are used with candidates.

## Learning through Karat

From March to December 2024, I worked remotely as a freelance Interview Engineer (IVE) at Karat.
The opportunity came through LinkedIn, and I had previously encountered its process as a candidate.

I valued the onboarding, training materials and structured approach to conducting interviews and
recording evaluations. That experience strengthened my appreciation for consistent processes, while
also reinforcing my preference for adapting assessments to the specific work and client context.
That preference for greater flexibility contributed to my decision to leave the engagement.

## Preparing interviewers and interviewer trainers

I created a training process that starts with observing my interviews, followed by conducting an
interview while I observe and coach. I developed TalentLMS training, articles and supporting materials
covering interview conduct, evidence, feedback quality and bias, including language-related bias.
I also train interviewer trainers and prepare selected interviewers for specialized roles.

Many participants are practicing engineers taking on interviewing alongside their delivery work.
Training needs to be quick to adopt and practical enough for them to produce useful, consistent
feedback. I track invitations, acceptance, onboarding and interview performance, and have also
coached more than 200 candidates.

## Connecting assessments, systems and people

I configure hiring workflows across assessment platforms, AI interviewing tools and applicant tracking
systems, including HackerRank, Coderbyte, Alex AI, Ashby, Greenhouse and Teamtailor. This work includes
forms, shared evaluation templates, low-code and no-code workflows, vendor evaluation, bug reports and
feature suggestions.

One workflow connected an Alex AI interview with a HackerRank take-home assessment and the talent
team's applicant tracking process. I also produce reports and organize interviewer availability so
that the process works beyond the individual assessment.

## Using evidence and AI to improve the process

I defined feedback quality standards and use AI-assisted analysis to review assessments, interview
feedback and client responses. I track outcomes from interview approval through client review,
offers and hires to identify inconsistencies and improve calibration.

When client feedback lacks useful detail, the process flags the need for clarification. Comparing
that feedback with the interview and CV can reveal recurring rejection patterns or a mismatch in
expectations that needs a direct conversation with the client.

I helped develop a workflow that uses interview transcripts, prompts and agents to organize evidence,
with references to the relevant moments in the recording. Human reviewers verify the facts and
evidence. AI supports preparation and analysis; specialist validation and human judgment remain part
of the process.

## Adapting interviews as AI changes engineering work

I have increasingly encountered candidates whose recent work consists largely of reviewing
AI-generated code, with less hands-on implementation. This raises questions about how we assess
writing, reviewing, debugging and understanding code as distinct skills.

Candidates also ask what AI tools companies provide. One candidate asked whether the company would
supply tokens or an AI environment—a question I consider entirely reasonable. In my writing, I also
question the contradiction of advertising an AI-first role while prohibiting AI during the interview.
Assessments should explain which independent skills they need to verify and where AI-assisted work
reflects the actual job.

Another candidate described being hired to “unvibe code” an application a founder had built six months
earlier. The anecdote points to a potentially growing need for engineers who can understand, validate
and maintain AI-generated applications beyond the initial prototype.

## Helping candidates communicate their experience

Capable engineers can struggle to explain complex technical ideas in English. This informs my
attention to language-related bias and the distinction between technical understanding and language
fluency, while considering each role's communication requirements.

I encourage candidates to prepare and practice explaining their background, projects and engineering
decisions. Drawing on advice from one of my own English teachers, I recommend preparing for
predictable questions so candidates do not have to formulate every answer from scratch during the
interview.
